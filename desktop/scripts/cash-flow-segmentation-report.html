What changed?

The interval score combines full width with a penalty for observations outside the band. At the nominal 80% target, the penalty is ten times the outside distance. Lower is better; a negative improvement means a worse score.

The two tails are measured separately. Symmetric cash bands need not produce equal upside and downside miss rates.

Why tangible book value / EBIT is an imperfect asset-intensity measure

The ratio is book equity per annual operating profit. It combines capital intensity, the profit margin and financing. It can be a useful predictor to test, but it does not identify physical asset requirements by itself.

Separate the economic questions

  • Asset intensity: average PP&E or tangible operating capital relative to sales.
  • Operating returns: normalized NOPAT relative to average operating capital.
  • Financing: gross debt, liquidity, interest coverage and maturities.
  • Predictability: demand, margins, cash stability and investment timing.

Book values need context

  • Internally developed brands and research can be absent from recognized assets.
  • Leased assets and debt need a consistent perimeter.
  • Depreciated old equipment can look unusually capital-light.
  • Acquisition cost still matters when goodwill is excluded from operating diagnostics.

Sources: Berkshire on tangible returns and acquisition cost, IAS 38 on intangibles, IFRS 16 on leases.

Calibration, limits and reproducible files

    The publication gates prevent a learned range being applied before its nominal calibration date, but later-vintage statement values cannot recreate the original historical information set. Retained company directories can omit former or delisted issuers, and missing outcomes are absent from the scored cohort. An 80% empirical target is neither a coverage guarantee for an individual company nor a probability interval for its DCF valuation. Signed annual cash paths and terminal assumptions require separate analysis.

    Further research: study the cash-flow components and business mechanisms behind misses. Barth, Cram and Nelson's original study supports testing accrual components alongside cash; IAS 7 distinguishes operations from investment and financing. Net investing is not a universal capex measure.
